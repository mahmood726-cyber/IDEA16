# Are ICD Meta-Analyses in Non-Ischemic Cardiomyopathy Obsolete?

**Lessons from the Aspirin Primary Prevention Paradigm Shift**

A critical methodological analysis arguing that meta-analyses of implantable
cardioverter-defibrillators (ICDs) in non-ischemic cardiomyopathy (NICM) may have
been rendered obsolete by the rise of modern guideline-directed medical therapy
(ARNi, SGLT2i), which has substantially lowered baseline sudden-cardiac-death
risk — mirroring how statin-era aspirin trials (ARRIVE, ASCEND, ASPREE) overturned
pre-statin meta-analyses.

**Core finding:** Applying the historical 23% relative risk reduction to modern
baseline SCD rates raises the number-needed-to-treat from ~33–54 to ~66 (a
22–100% increase), suggesting the historical evidence base reflects a therapeutic
era that no longer exists.

## Nature of this work

This is a **narrative/methodological critique and quantitative re-analysis**, not
a registered systematic review. The numbers are derived from published trial
summary statistics and clearly-stated simulations (applying a fixed RRR to modern
baseline risks); see the manuscript's Methods for assumptions and limitations.
Hypothesis-generating — it argues for new RCTs in contemporary GDMT populations.
